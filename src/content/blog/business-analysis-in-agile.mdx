## Why Business Analysts matter in Agile teams

In traditional waterfall projects, the BA role is clear: gather requirements, write a spec, hand it off. In Agile, that handoff model breaks down. Requirements evolve with every sprint, and the BA must evolve alongside them.

## The core artefacts

### Business Requirements Document (BRD)

The BRD captures *what* the business needs — goals, constraints, and success criteria — independent of any technical solution. It is the contract between the business and the delivery team.

### Functional Requirements Document (FRD)

The FRD translates the BRD into *how* the system should behave. User stories, acceptance criteria, data flows, and edge cases all live here.

## Working with Scrum

In a healthy Scrum team, the BA works closely with the Product Owner to:

1. **Refine the backlog** — turning vague ideas into well-defined user stories.
2. **Define acceptance criteria** — so QA and developers share a single definition of done.
3. **Facilitate stakeholder sessions** — bridging the language gap between technical and non-technical stakeholders.

## Conclusion

The value of a Business Analyst in Agile is not about writing documents — it is about creating shared understanding. When everyone on the team knows *why* they are building something, quality and velocity both improve.
